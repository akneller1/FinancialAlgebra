Essential vocabulary

Cost of attendance (COA)
The total estimated yearly cost of college: tuition, fees, housing, food, books, transportation, and personal expenses
Sticker price
The published cost of attendance before any financial aid
Net price
Cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships — what the family must actually pay or borrow
Grant
Financial aid, usually need-based, that does not have to be repaid
Scholarship
Gift aid, often merit-based, that does not have to be repaid
Work-study
A financial aid program that provides part-time jobs to help students earn money for college costs
Subsidized loan
A federal student loan for which the government pays the interest while the student is enrolled at least half-time
Unsubsidized loan
A student loan that accrues interest from the time it is disbursed, including during school
Capitalized interest
Unpaid interest that is added to the loan principal, so future interest is charged on it
FAFSA
The Free Application for Federal Student Aid, used to determine eligibility for federal grants, loans, and work-study
Return on investment (ROI)
The gain from an investment compared with its cost, often expressed as a percent or a payback period
Payback period
The time needed for extra earnings to cover the cost of an investment
Opportunity cost of education
Earnings given up while in school instead of working full-time
Apprenticeship
A paid training program combining on-the-job learning with instruction, leading to a credential
Lifetime earnings
The total income earned over a working career
Completion rate
The share of students who finish a program within a set time

Common misconceptions

  • Loans reduce the price.
  • College is always/never worth it.
  • Sticker price = what you pay.
  • Starting salary is the only number that matters.

Worked example

  1. COA $30,500 − grants $13,000 = $17,500 net
  2. Borrow $5,500 × 4 = $22,000 at 6.5%, 10 yr
  3. Payment ≈ $249.81/month
  4. Starting salary $55,000 → payments ≈ 5.5% of gross

Questions to ask before deciding

  • What is the net price for me?
  • How much will I borrow in total?
  • What do graduates of this program earn?
  • What is the completion rate?
  • What are my alternatives (transfer, apprenticeship, certificate)?

Decision tips

  • File the FAFSA every year.
  • Compare net prices, not sticker prices.
  • Borrow no more than your expected first-year salary.
  • Pay interest during school if you can.
