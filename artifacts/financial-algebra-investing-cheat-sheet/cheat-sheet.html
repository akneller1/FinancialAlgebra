Financial Algebra · Unit 6: Investing & Compound Growth

Essential vocabulary

Compound growth
Growth in which earnings are added to the balance and then earn returns themselves
Rate of return
The gain or loss on an investment over a period, expressed as a percent of the starting value
Rule of 72
A shortcut estimating years to double: 72 divided by the annual percent return
Recurring contribution
A regular, repeated deposit into an investment, such as monthly
Future value
The value of money or a series of payments at a future date given a rate of return
Dollar-cost averaging
Investing a fixed dollar amount on a regular schedule regardless of price
Stock
A share of ownership in a company whose value can rise or fall
Bond
A loan to a government or company that pays interest and returns principal at maturity
Index fund
A fund that holds the investments in a market index to match its performance at low cost
Expense ratio
The yearly fee a fund charges, as a percent of the amount invested
Diversification
Spreading money across many investments so that one loss has less impact
Risk
The possibility that actual returns differ from expected returns, including losing money
Volatility
How much an investment’s value moves up and down over time
Time horizon
How long until the money is needed
Risk tolerance
A person’s ability and willingness to accept investment losses
Real return
Return after adjusting for inflation

Common misconceptions

  • High average returns are guaranteed.
  • Doubling the rate doubles the result.
  • Small fees do not matter.
  • Diversification eliminates risk.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • When will I need this money?
  • What could I lose in a bad year?
  • What are the fees?
  • Am I diversified?
  • Have I built an emergency fund and paid off high-interest debt first?

Decision tips

  • Start early, even small.
  • Automate contributions.
  • Prefer low-cost diversified funds.
  • Don’t panic-sell in downturns if your horizon is long.
