Credit Cards & Debt — Cheat Sheet

Financial Algebra · Unit 4: Credit Cards & Debt

Essential vocabulary

APR (annual percentage rate)
The yearly cost of borrowing expressed as a percent, used to compute interest on a balance
Periodic rate
The APR divided into the rate for one billing period or day
Balance
The amount owed on an account at a given time
Grace period
The time between the end of a billing cycle and the due date during which new purchases are not charged interest if the full balance is paid
Average daily balance
A method of computing interest by averaging the balance owed on each day of the billing cycle
Minimum payment
The smallest payment required by the due date to keep the account in good standing
Credit limit
The maximum balance a lender allows on a credit account
Credit utilization
The percent of available revolving credit currently being used
Credit score
A number (commonly 300–850) summarizing credit risk based on payment history, amounts owed, length of history, new credit, and credit mix
Credit report
A record of a person’s credit accounts and payment history kept by credit bureaus
Late fee
A charge for paying less than the minimum by the due date
Debt avalanche
A repayment strategy that sends extra money to the debt with the highest interest rate first
Debt snowball
A repayment strategy that sends extra money to the smallest balance first for quick wins
Balance transfer
Moving a balance to another card, often at a low promotional rate with a transfer fee
Cash advance
Withdrawing cash using a credit card, usually with a fee, a higher APR, and no grace period
Revolving credit
Credit that can be borrowed, repaid, and borrowed again up to a limit

Common misconceptions

  • APR is charged monthly as-is.
  • Minimum payment is the recommended payment.
  • Carrying a balance builds credit.
  • 0% transfer offers are always free.

Worked example

  1. $3,000 at 24.99% APR
  2. First month interest = 3,000 × 0.249912 = $62.48
  3. Minimum (1% + interest) = $92.48
  4. Fixed $150/month → about 26 months; minimum-only → many years

Questions to ask before deciding

  • What is the APR — and what is the monthly rate?
  • Can I pay the full balance by the due date?
  • What fees apply (annual, late, cash advance, transfer)?
  • How long will repayment take at my planned payment?
  • How will this affect my utilization?

Decision tips

  • Pay the full statement balance whenever possible.
  • Set autopay for at least the minimum to avoid late fees.
  • Keep utilization low.
  • Avoid cash advances.
