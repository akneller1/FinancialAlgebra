Financial Algebra · Unit 5: Auto Loans & Borrowing

Essential vocabulary

Installment loan
A loan repaid in equal scheduled payments over a fixed term
Loan term
The length of time to repay a loan
Down payment
Cash paid up front that reduces the amount borrowed
Amortization
Paying off a loan through regular payments that cover interest first and then principal
Amortization schedule
A table showing each payment’s interest, principal, and remaining balance
Finance charge
The total dollar cost of credit, including interest and certain fees
Total of payments
The sum of all scheduled payments over the life of a loan
Upside down (negative equity)
Owing more on a loan than the item is worth
Depreciation
The decline in an asset’s value over time
Lease
A contract to use a vehicle for a set term and mileage in exchange for payments, without owning it
Residual value
The estimated value of a leased vehicle at the end of the lease
Money factor
The lease version of an interest rate; multiply by 2,400 to estimate an equivalent APR
Collateral
An asset pledged to secure a loan that the lender can take if payments stop
Cosigner
A person who agrees to repay a loan if the borrower does not
Prepayment
Paying more than the required payment to reduce the principal faster
Total cost of ownership
All costs of having a vehicle over time: price or payments, interest, insurance, fuel, maintenance, fees, and lost value

Common misconceptions

  • Lowest payment = cheapest loan.
  • Interest is the same every month.
  • Add-ons cost only their sticker price.
  • Loan approval = affordability.

  2. 48 months: $525.81/month → total $25,238.88

  4. Longer term costs about $2,551 more

Questions to ask before deciding

  • What is the out-the-door price?
  • What APR do I qualify for elsewhere?
  • What is the total of payments?
  • How long until I owe less than the car is worth?
  • What will insurance, fuel, and maintenance cost?

Decision tips

  • Get preapproved before visiting the dealer.
  • Negotiate price, then APR, then term.
  • Avoid financing add-ons.
  • Keep the term as short as your budget allows.
